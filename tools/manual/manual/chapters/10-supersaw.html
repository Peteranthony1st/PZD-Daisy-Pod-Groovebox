Supersaw

The Supersaw is modelled on the Roland JP-8000's Super Saw voice: seven detuned saws with the JP's own detune spacing, knob curve and mix. Around them are a second oscillator, a resonant filter per voice with its own envelope, an LFO, mono and legato modes with portamento, velocity, and a stereo chorus. It has 42 factory sounds, from trance leads and plucks to future-bass chords and synthwave brass. Open it from Global:SSaw.

The SSaw page

K1 is Detune and K2 is Mix. The page draws the seven saws as bars: they spread out as you turn Detune, with the JP's uneven spacing, and their height follows Mix. The centre saw is solid and the outer pair dotted.

Spread shows how far the outer saws are from the centre one, in cents. The JP's Detune climbs slowly, then steeply at the top, to ±202 cents at 100%. Mix raises the six side saws from almost silent to louder than the centre one.

Playing it

  • Velocity (MIDI page, B2 then K1): harder than the JP's standard velocity of 80 is louder, brighter (the cutoff opens and the filter envelope sweeps further) and snappier. Softer is quieter, darker and slower to swell.
  • The LFO is one for the whole engine, so a synced one stays on the beat with the looper. Pitch has a Fade for each note.
  • Mono plays the last key held and goes back to the one still down when you let go. Legato doesn't restart the envelopes on an overlapping note. Portamento glides over its time, in Poly too.
  • User saves go in SUPERSAW/.
Processor. At its heaviest (ten voices sounding, full Detune and Mix, OSC2, 24 dB filter, pitch and filter LFOs) the Supersaw takes about 18–19% of the processor. Everyday sounds take less.
