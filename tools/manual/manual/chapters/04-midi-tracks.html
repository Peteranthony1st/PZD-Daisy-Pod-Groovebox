MIDI tracks

The eight MIDI tracks, M1–M8, record what you play on a keyboard and play it back through any of the eight engines. They sit on Home after the audio tracks and work the same way: hold B1 to record a pass, tap to pause, hold again to overdub. Each has eight patterns, a chain to play them in order, quantise and swing, and a grid for drawing and editing notes.

Recording a MIDI track

  1. On Home, turn past A4 to a MIDI track. While it's selected, the keyboard plays its engine.
  2. Hold B1. After the count-in, play: the track records one pass of its length, then loops it.
  3. Tap B1 to pause or play. Hold B1 again to overdub more notes on top.

The engine has to be switched on (on its Global page) for you to hear it. A track's notes are saved with the Performance.

A track's pages

Click the encoder on a MIDI track to open its pages, and turn to move between them. A click goes back to Home. B1 records, pauses and overdubs on every page, as on Home.

Track

K1 picks the engine the track plays. K2 sets its length: ¼, ½, 1, 2 or 4 times the loop, up to 16 bars, so a one-bar beat can repeat under a four-bar loop. Tap B2 to undo the last pass; hold it to clear the track.

Patt

Each track has eight patterns, A–H, each with its own notes, length, quantise, swing and transpose. K1 picks a pattern and K2 transposes it, ±24 semitones. Tap B2 to play the picked pattern: it takes over when the one playing reaches its end. Hold B2 to copy the playing pattern into the picked one, to start a variation.

Chain

A chain plays up to eight patterns in order, each for its own length: A A A B is three bars of A, then a fill. K1 picks a pattern and a B2 tap adds it to the chain; hold B2 to clear the chain. A chain starts with the loop.

Arp

Every pattern has its own arpeggiator. K1 picks a pattern and B1 switches its arp on or off; click the encoder to edit it (see Chapter 6). Hold B2 to copy the picked pattern's arp to every pattern.

Quant

K1 sets the quantise, from Off through 1/32T to 1/4, and K2 the swing, up to 100%. Both apply on playback: the notes you recorded never move, so you can change your mind.

Print

Records the track into an audio track, to free the engine or keep a take. K1 picks the audio track; K2 picks This track or every MIDI track (Tracks). Hold B2 to print, and tap it to stop. The MIDI tracks pause afterwards. Past the audio tracks, K1 reaches Notes: holding B2 then writes the arp's notes into the track's next empty pattern as real, editable notes.

The grid

After Print come the grid pages, one per bar. Most engines get a piano roll, C1 to C6; a track playing SMPDR gets a drum grid instead, with one row per pad from C2.

K1 moves along the steps and K2 picks the note. B1 adds or deletes a note, which sounds as it's added. Hold B2 to set the note's velocity (on a drum grid) or length with K1, and its roll with K2: ×2 to ×8 quick repeats inside one step. With K1 fully left, an edit applies to the whole row.

If the track's arp is on, its notes show over the keys you recorded: a hollow bar where it plays a note, a line through a recorded key it plays.
